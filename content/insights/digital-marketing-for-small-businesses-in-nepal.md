Most small business owners do not lack opinions about digital marketing; they lack a starting point. There are many channels, many agencies, and a constant sense that everyone else is doing more. This guide gives you a way to start: decide what you want, pick a small number of channels, run a simple plan for 30 days and measure the right things.

It does not promise a result, and it does not quote costs, because both depend on your business and market. It will help you ask better questions, whether you do the work yourself or hire help.

## Start with a business goal, not a channel

"We should be on TikTok" is a channel. "We need more weekday bookings" is a goal. Starting with the goal changes every later decision. Typical goals for a small business are:

- More **enquiries** (calls, messages, form submissions).
- More **walk-ins or bookings** at a physical location.
- More **online orders**.
- Greater **awareness** in a new area or among a new customer group.

Choose one primary goal for the next three months. Marketing that tries to achieve everything at once usually measures nothing.

## Know who you are talking to

Write down, in a few sentences, who your best customers are: where they live, what they need, how they currently find businesses like yours, and what makes them hesitate. A hotel guesthouse in Pokhara, a clothing seller in Kathmandu and an accounting firm are all "small businesses", but they reach customers in entirely different ways. If you cannot describe the customer, no channel will fix that.

## Choose channels where your customers already are

You do not need every channel. For most small businesses, two or three are enough to start. The main options and what they are generally good for:

| Channel | Generally good for | What it asks of you |
|---|---|---|
| **Google Search and Maps** (including your Google Business Profile) | People actively looking for a business like yours nearby | An accurate, complete profile, good photos, reviews, and a website that answers questions |
| **Facebook and Instagram** | Showing your products, people and place; reaching a defined local audience with ads | Regular, genuinely useful content; a fast response to messages |
| **TikTok and short-form video** | Reaching new audiences with entertaining or educational video | Consistent video production; patience, since reach is unpredictable |
| **YouTube** | Longer explanations, tours, how-tos, brand films | Higher production effort; content that stays useful over time |
| **WhatsApp / Messenger** | Turning interest into conversations and bookings | Quick, polite replies and a clear process |
| **Email / SMS** | Staying in touch with people who already know you | A list you built with permission |

Many Nepali consumer businesses find that Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube are where their customers spend time, but verify that for **your** customers by asking them directly. A short question at checkout or on a booking form ("How did you hear about us?") is one of the cheapest forms of research you can run.

## Get the basics right before spending on ads

Paid advertising amplifies what you already have. If the basics are weak, ads simply pay to send people to a weak experience. Check these first:

1. **Your Google Business Profile** is claimed, accurate and complete: name, category, address or service area, phone, hours, photos and services. Google's own help pages explain how it works.
2. **Your name, address and phone number are consistent** everywhere they appear: website, social profiles, directories.
3. **You have a clear way to contact you** that works on a phone: a tap-to-call number, a WhatsApp link, a short form.
4. **Your website or page loads quickly on mobile** and says what you do, where, and for whom within the first screen.
5. **You respond to messages and reviews** promptly and politely.

If you have a website, Google's SEO Starter Guide is a clear, free explanation of what helps people find it.

## Organic and paid: how to think about both

**Organic** marketing is content and presence you do not pay to show: posts, videos, your profile, reviews, search visibility. It compounds slowly and costs time and production effort.

**Paid** marketing buys attention: ads on Meta platforms, Google and others. It works faster and can be switched off, but stops when the budget stops.

A sensible pattern for most small businesses is to build a small base of good organic content and a complete Google profile first, then test paid advertising with a small budget on one clear offer. Both Meta and Google publish help documentation on how their ad systems work and what their policies require; read the policies before you start, since rejected or restricted ads are a common early frustration.

## A simple 30-day starting plan

**Week 1: Foundations**
- Write down your goal, your customer and your single main offer.
- Claim or fix your Google Business Profile; check your social profile details.
- Set up a way to track enquiries (see below).

**Week 2: Content**
- Plan 8–12 pieces of content that show your product, your people, your process or answers to common questions. Quality and usefulness matter more than quantity.
- Take or commission good photos and a few short videos.

**Week 3: Publish and respond**
- Publish consistently; reply to every message and comment the same day where you can.
- Ask recent happy customers for an honest review.

**Week 4: Review and decide**
- Look at what happened: which posts led to messages, which channels brought real enquiries.
- Decide what to repeat, what to drop, and whether a small paid test makes sense.

## Measure what matters

It is easy to track numbers that look good and mean little. Prioritise in this order:

1. **Business results:** enquiries, bookings, orders, sales attributable to marketing.
2. **Cost of a result:** how much you spent to get an enquiry or a customer.
3. **Quality of enquiries:** how many were serious.
4. **Reach and engagement:** useful for diagnosing problems, but not a goal by themselves.

Likes, followers and views are not results. A smaller account that produces ten real enquiries a month is more valuable than a large one that produces none.

To measure, you do not need complicated tools at first. A simple sheet that records each enquiry, its date and how the person found you is enough to learn a great deal in a month.

## Common mistakes

- **Spreading too thin.** Five channels done poorly lose to two done well.
- **Posting without a purpose.** Every post should help a customer decide something.
- **Boosting posts without a goal.** Define the audience and the action you want before spending.
- **Ignoring messages.** Slow replies waste the attention you paid for.
- **Expecting overnight results.** Most channels take months to compound.
- **Not tracking.** If you cannot tell what produced an enquiry, you cannot improve.

## When it makes sense to hire help

You may reach a point where the time cost of doing it yourself outweighs the cost of help: when you have a clear goal and offer but not the time or skills for content production, advertising or measurement. When you do, prepare a short brief first; our guide to [writing a creative brief](/insights/advertising/how-to-write-a-creative-brief-for-an-ad-or-video) works for marketing projects as well as ads. Ask any agency to explain what they will do, what they will report and how they define success, and be cautious of anyone who guarantees rankings or results.
